## Two findings

**1. Full training does not fix the degenerate operating point.** Across all 16 validation evals, 15
had recall 0.0 at tau = 1.0. **Exactly one** (update 700) reached a real graded low-FP point —
**recall 0.014 at FPR 0.9%** (tau 0.49) — proving the model *can* occasionally produce a non-degenerate
tail, but it is **unstable and, even at its best, below the NLI baseline's 0.073 at its
validation-selected threshold** (16/883 false accepts; 1.81% realized internal-test FPR). So the
model has **not** demonstrably beaten the baseline at the target operating point.

**2. The `_pauc` implementation is buggy — it over-credits the degenerate tail.** When the first
false-positive operating point lands *beyond* `fpr_max`, `_pauc` linearly interpolates recall across
the whole `[0, fmax]` band, crediting recall the model never achieves at low FPR. An all-or-nothing
"reject everything (tau=1) then accept a burst" curve scores pAUC ≈ **0.5**, while the genuinely
graded update-700 curve scored **0.275**. So pAUC-selection picked the **degenerate** update-300
(pAUC 0.5) over the **better** update-700 — the metric is inverted in exactly the regime it was meant
to fix. The correct behavior is a step interpolation (hold the last recall reached at fpr ≤ fmax; do
not credit recall past fmax).

## Correction to M3-REPORT.md

M3-REPORT.md cited "pAUC 0.097 vs NLI 0.048 (~2× low-FP signal)" as a promising sign. **That
comparison is unreliable** — it used the same buggy `_pauc`. The pAUC numbers in both M3 and M3.1
reports are **invalid** and were replaced by the M4 recomputation. The update-700 observation is a
bounded validation-checkpoint diagnostic (recall 0.014 at 0.9% validation FPR), not evidence of
final internal-test performance. Calibration ECE 0.02–0.04 is bug-independent, but it does not
establish useful discrimination for a collapsed score distribution.

## Disposition + recommendation — stop paid tweaks; fix the measurement (M4, $0 first)

The historical decision was that two paid runs were confounded by measurement and selection, so
iterating further before fixing the metric would waste money. **The next milestone was a $0,
no-GPU measurement and analysis pass** before any further training:

1. **Fix `_pauc`** (step interpolation; no recall credited past `fmax`) + a unit test on a synthetic
   all-or-nothing curve (must score ≈ 0, not 0.5).
2. **Re-examine the low-FP operating point.** Recall@FPR≤1% is pinned by ~8 of 863 validation
   negatives — too brittle. Options: a larger validation negative pool, a wider *validated* FP budget,
   or a precision-target threshold. This is a task-definition decision for the owner.
3. **Investigate the training dynamics** — the logged step-loss oscillates (≈0.03 at epoch ends,
   ≈1.38 mid-epoch), worth understanding before the next paid run.
4. Only then, one corrected paid run (M3.2) with the fixed metric + operating point.
